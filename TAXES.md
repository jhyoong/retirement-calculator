# Singapore Taxes Reference (Individuals)

Last updated: 14 Nov 2026

This file supports the retirement calculator. It covers personal income tax, property tax and property stamp duties for individuals. Figures come from IRAS, MOF and the Singapore Budget site unless marked "unverified".

Notes on terms:
- YA = Year of Assessment. Tax for YA N is based on income earned in calendar year N-1. Example: YA 2026 covers income from 1 Jan 2025 to 31 Dec 2025.
- AV = Annual Value of a property (used for property tax).
- SC = Singapore Citizen, SPR = Singapore Permanent Resident.

---

## 1. Personal Income Tax

### 1.1 Resident tax rates (YA 2024 onwards, including YA 2026)

Tax is applied to chargeable income (income after expenses, donations and reliefs).

| Chargeable income ($) | Rate | Tax on this band ($) | Total tax at top of band ($) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 - 20,000 | 0% | 0 | 0 |
| 20,001 - 30,000 | 2% | 200 | 200 |
| 30,001 - 40,000 | 3.5% | 350 | 550 |
| 40,001 - 80,000 | 7% | 2,800 | 3,350 |
| 80,001 - 120,000 | 11.5% | 4,600 | 7,950 |
| 120,001 - 160,000 | 15% | 6,000 | 13,950 |
| 160,001 - 200,000 | 18% | 7,200 | 21,150 |
| 200,001 - 240,000 | 19% | 7,600 | 28,750 |
| 240,001 - 280,000 | 19.5% | 7,800 | 36,550 |
| 280,001 - 320,000 | 20% | 8,000 | 44,550 |
| 320,001 - 500,000 | 22% | 39,600 | 84,150 |
| 500,001 - 1,000,000 | 23% | 115,000 | 199,150 |
| Above 1,000,000 | 24% | - | - |

### 1.2 YA 2027 changes

- Budget 2026 (12 Feb 2026, Annex H-1 Tax Changes) contains no change to resident personal income tax rates. The table above continues to apply for YA 2027 unless a later announcement changes it.
- Changes affecting individuals from YA 2027 that were found:
  - CPF Cash Top-up Relief: cash top-ups to MediSave that attract the Matched MediSave Scheme (MMSS) grant no longer qualify (top-ups made from 1 Jan 2026). See section 2.3.
  - Course Fees Relief ended from YA 2026. MOF has said there are no plans to reintroduce it.
  - The 250% tax deduction for qualifying donations is extended to donations made from 1 Jan 2027 to 31 Dec 2029 (Budget 2026 Annex H-1).

### 1.3 Tax residency

You are a tax resident for a YA if, in the previous calendar year:
- You are an SC or SPR who normally lives in Singapore, except for temporary absences; or
- You are a foreigner who stayed or worked in Singapore for at least 183 days (the 183-day rule).

Concessions for foreigners:
- Three-year concession: a foreigner who stays or works in Singapore continuously for 3 consecutive years is a resident for all 3 years, even if the first and third years are under 183 days.
- Two-year concession: a foreign employee whose employment spans 2 calendar years, with a total stay of at least 183 days (including presence just before and after employment), is a resident for both years. Does not apply to company directors, public entertainers or professionals.

### 1.4 Non-resident tax rates (YA 2024 onwards)

| Income type | Rate |
|---|---|
| Employment income | Flat 15% or the resident progressive rates, whichever gives the higher tax |
| Director's fees, consultation fees and all other income (e.g. rental, pension) | 24% (22% before YA 2024) |
| SRS withdrawals by foreign SRS account holders (withholding tax) | 24%, or 15% concessionary rate if conditions are met |

No personal reliefs are available to non-residents.

### 1.5 Personal Income Tax Rebates

| YA | Rebate | Source |
|---|---|---|
| YA 2024 | 50% of tax payable, capped at $200 | IRAS |
| YA 2025 | 60% of tax payable, capped at $200 (Budget 2025, SG60 package) | IRAS, MOF |
| YA 2026 | None. Budget 2026 Annex H-1 contains no personal income tax rebate. The IRAS rebate page lists none for YA 2026. | MOF Budget 2026 Annex H-1 |
| YA 2027 | None announced as of the date of this file | - |

Rebate rules (YA 2025): only for tax residents; given automatically; cannot be carried forward; no cash payout if tax payable is $0.

Note: The YA 2026 50% rebate capped at $40,000 is a Corporate Income Tax rebate for companies. It does not apply to individuals.

## 2. Tax Reliefs Relevant to Retirement Planning

### 2.1 Personal relief cap

- Total personal reliefs claimed in a YA are capped at $80,000. This includes SRS relief and CPF Cash Top-up Relief.

### 2.2 Earned Income Relief

Based on age at 31 Dec of the year before the YA. Capped at your actual earned income (less allowable expenses) if that is lower.

| Age | Earned Income Relief | Earned Income Relief (Disability) |
|---|---|---|
| Below 55 | $1,000 | $4,000 |
| 55 to 59 | $6,000 | $10,000 |
| 60 and above | $8,000 | $12,000 |

### 2.3 CPF Relief (compulsory contributions)

- Employees (SC/SPR only) get relief for compulsory employee CPF contributions on wages up to the CPF ceilings.
- Ordinary Wage (OW) monthly ceiling: $7,400 in 2025; $8,000 from 1 Jan 2026.
- Annual salary ceiling: $102,000 (unchanged).
- Additional Wage (AW) ceiling: $102,000 minus total OW subject to CPF for the year.
- Self-employed persons have a separate CPF relief (for MediSave contributions). Details not covered here.

Derived figure (not stated directly by IRAS on the page read): for an employee aged 55 and below at the 20% employee rate, the most relief possible is about 20% x $102,000 = $20,400. Employee rates are lower for older workers. Check `CPF_INFO.md` for age-based contribution rates.

### 2.4 CPF Cash Top-up Relief

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Maximum relief per YA | $16,000 total: up to $8,000 for top-ups to own accounts, up to $8,000 for top-ups to family members |
| Who can claim | SC or SPR |
| Eligible accounts (self) | Special Account (below 55), Retirement Account (55 and above), MediSave Account (from YA 2023) |
| Eligible family members | Parents, grandparents (incl. in-laws), spouse, siblings. Spouse and non-disabled siblings must have annual income of $8,000 or less in the prior year ($4,000 for YA 2024 and earlier) |
| SA / RA cap | Relief only on top-ups up to the Full Retirement Sum (FRS). RA top-ups can go up to the Enhanced Retirement Sum, but relief stops at FRS |
| MA cap | Relief only on top-ups up to the Basic Healthcare Sum (BHS) |
| Not eligible | Transfers from your own CPF accounts; top-ups that attract the Matched Retirement Savings Scheme (MRSS) grant (from YA 2026, top-ups from 1 Jan 2025); MediSave top-ups that attract the Matched MediSave Scheme (MMSS) grant (from YA 2027, top-ups from 1 Jan 2026) |

FRS and BHS used for relief:

| YA | Top-up year | FRS | BHS |
|---|---|---|---|
| YA 2026 | 2025 | $213,000 | $75,500 |
| YA 2027 | 2026 | $220,400 | $79,000 |

BHS is fixed for life once a member turns 65.

Before YA 2023 the relief was $7,000 per category and covered SA/RA only.

### 2.5 Supplementary Retirement Scheme (SRS)

Contribution caps (per year), and relief:

| Account holder | Annual contribution cap |
|---|---|
| SC and SPR | $15,300 |
| Foreigner | $35,700 |

- Relief equals the amount contributed in the calendar year, given in the following YA, if you are a tax resident for that YA.
- No relief if the account is suspended on 31 Dec, or for amounts withdrawn in the same year.

Withdrawal taxation:

| Situation | Tax treatment |
|---|---|
| Withdrawal on or after the statutory retirement age that applied at the time of your first SRS contribution | 50% of the amount withdrawn is taxable. No penalty |
| Early withdrawal (before that age) | 100% taxable, plus a 5% penalty on the amount withdrawn |
| Exceptions to the 5% penalty | Death, medical grounds, bankruptcy, and full withdrawal by a foreigner who has held the account for at least 10 years since first contribution (and was not an SC/SPR in that time) |

- 10-year window: penalty-free withdrawals can be spread over 10 years starting from the date of the first penalty-free withdrawal. The account is deemed closed in the 10th year of this period. IRAS states that any balance at that point is treated as withdrawn (this "deemed withdrawal" detail is commonly cited; the exact wording was not read on the IRAS page, so treat it as unverified).
- IRAS example: with no other taxable income, withdrawing $40,000 a year is tax-free (50% = $20,000, which falls in the 0% band), giving up to $400,000 tax-free over 10 years.
- Statutory retirement age: rises from 63 to 64 from 1 Jul 2026 (IRAS). A later rise does not affect people who already made their first contribution. The age was 62 before it became 63; the date of that change (1 Jul 2022) is unverified from an official source in this research.

### 2.6 Spouse, Parent and Child Reliefs (YA 2026)

Spouse Relief:

| Relief | Amount |
|---|---|
| Spouse Relief | $2,000 |
| Spouse Relief (Disability) | $5,500 |

- Spouse's annual income in the prior year must be $8,000 or less (from YA 2025; $4,000 before). Not required for the disability relief.
- Not available to people paying alimony to a former spouse. Legally separated spouses paying maintenance under a court order can claim the lower of maintenance paid or the relief amount.

Parent Relief (per dependant, up to 2 dependants):

| Situation | Parent Relief | Parent Relief (Disability) |
|---|---|---|
| Living with you | $9,000 | $14,000 |
| Not living with you (you spent at least $2,000 supporting them) | $5,500 | $10,000 |

- Dependant must be 55 or older and have annual income of $8,000 or less (from YA 2025; $4,000 before). These two conditions do not apply for disability relief.
- "Annual income" includes tax-exempt income such as bank interest and dividends, but excludes CPF payouts.

Qualifying Child Relief (QCR) and Working Mother's Child Relief (WMCR):

| Relief | Amount |
|---|---|
| QCR, per child | $4,000 |
| Child Relief (Disability), per child | $7,500 |
| WMCR, child born/adopted before 1 Jan 2024 | 15% / 20% / 25% of mother's earned income for 1st / 2nd / 3rd+ child |
| WMCR, child born/adopted on or after 1 Jan 2024 | $8,000 / $10,000 / $12,000 for 1st / 2nd / 3rd+ child |

- Child must be under 16, or studying full-time, and have annual income of $8,000 or less.
- QCR (or Child Relief (Disability)) plus WMCR is capped at $50,000 per child. Total WMCR is capped at 100% of the mother's earned income.

Other reliefs (NSman, life insurance, Grandparent Caregiver, etc.) were not researched for this file.

## 3. Tax Treatment of Retirement-Related Income

| Income | Taxable for resident individuals? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| CPF LIFE monthly payouts | No | IRAS: annuities received in Singapore are generally not taxable; CPF LIFE payouts are given as an example |
| CPF lump-sum withdrawals | No | CPF is listed by IRAS as a tax-exempt pension scheme/fund for lump-sum retirement benefits |
| Interest credited to CPF accounts | Not taxable (unverified) | No IRAS page read states this directly. It is widely understood to be tax-exempt. Interest on refunds of excess CPF contributions received before Apr 2025 was taxable |
| Dividends from Singapore-resident companies | No | One-tier system: company tax is final. Exception: dividends from co-operatives (e.g. NTUC co-ops) are taxable |
| REIT distributions | No | Unless received through a partnership or from a trade/business in REITs |
| Foreign dividends / foreign-sourced income received in Singapore | No | Unless received through a Singapore partnership |
| Interest from approved banks / licensed finance companies in Singapore | No | |
| Interest from bonds (debt securities) | No | Unless held by a partnership or as trading stock |
| Interest from non-approved banks, pawnshops, private loans | Yes | |
| Capital gains (shares, property, financial instruments held as personal investments) | No | Taxable if IRAS views the activity as trading ("badges of trade": frequency, intention, holding period, financing) |
| SRS withdrawals | Partly | See section 2.5 |
| Rental income | Yes | Net rent is added to other income and taxed at resident rates |

Rental income: 15% deemed expense rule (from YA 2016):
- For Singapore residential property, you can claim deemed expenses of 15% of gross rent instead of actual expenses (property tax, maintenance, minor repairs, etc.).
- Mortgage interest can be claimed on top of the 15%.
- You must use the same method (deemed or actual) for all your rented residential properties in the same YA. You can switch between years.
- Not available for non-residential property, rent received through a partnership or a trust, or where the only expense is mortgage interest.
- A rental loss cannot be set off against other income.
- Formula: Net rent = Gross rent - (15% x Gross rent) - Mortgage interest.

## 4. Property Tax

Property tax = Annual Value (AV) x applicable rate. Bills are issued in December for the following year and are due on 31 Jan.

### 4.1 How AV is determined

- AV is the estimated gross annual rent the property could earn if rented out, excluding furniture, furnishings and maintenance fees.
- It is based on market rents of similar properties, not the actual rent you receive. The property is valued as if vacant and available to let, even if it is owner-occupied.
- IRAS reviews residential AVs every year.
- IRAS example: a flat that could rent at $3,000 a month has an AV of $36,000.
- Vacant land: 5% of the estimated market value.

### 4.2 Owner-occupied residential rates (from 1 Jan 2025, still current for 2026)

Only one property, which the owner lives in, can get these rates.

| AV band ($) | Rate | Tax on band ($) | Total tax at top of band ($) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 - 12,000 | 0% | 0 | 0 |
| 12,001 - 40,000 | 4% | 1,120 | 1,120 |
| 40,001 - 50,000 | 6% | 600 | 1,720 |
| 50,001 - 75,000 | 10% | 2,500 | 4,220 |
| 75,001 - 85,000 | 14% | 1,400 | 5,620 |
| 85,001 - 100,000 | 20% | 3,000 | 8,620 |
| 100,001 - 140,000 | 26% | 10,400 | 19,020 |
| Above 140,000 | 32% | - | - |

AV band revision: announced at Budget 2024, all owner-occupier AV bands were raised from 1 Jan 2025. The 2024 table (for reference) was:

| AV band ($) | Rate |
|---|---|
| 0 - 8,000 | 0% |
| 8,001 - 30,000 | 4% |
| 30,001 - 40,000 | 6% |
| 40,001 - 55,000 | 10% |
| 55,001 - 70,000 | 14% |
| 70,001 - 85,000 | 20% |
| 85,001 - 100,000 | 26% |
| Above 100,000 | 32% |

No change to the bands or rates for 2026 or 2027 was found. IRAS's 2027 Property Tax Bill page mentions no rate change.

### 4.3 Owner-occupier property tax rebates

| Year | HDB flats (owner-occupied) | Private residential (owner-occupied) | Announced |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2025 | 20% | 15%, capped at $1,000 | 29 Nov 2024 |
| 2026 | 15% | 10%, capped at $500 | 28 Nov 2025 |
| 2027 | None announced as of 14 Nov 2026 | None announced | - |

Rebates are applied automatically against the bill. One-room and two-room HDB flats pay no property tax because their AV falls within the 0% band.

### 4.4 Non-owner-occupied residential rates (from 1 Jan 2024)

Applies to any residential property not occupied by its owner (rented out, vacant, or a second home).

| AV band ($) | Rate | Tax on band ($) | Total tax at top of band ($) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 - 30,000 | 12% | 3,600 | 3,600 |
| 30,001 - 45,000 | 20% | 3,000 | 6,600 |
| 45,001 - 60,000 | 28% | 4,200 | 10,800 |
| Above 60,000 | 36% | - | - |

For reference, the 2023 rates were 11% / 16% / 21% / 27% on the same bands.

### 4.5 Non-residential property

- Flat 10% of AV (commercial, industrial, land). Owner-occupier rates do not apply.
- Hotels, hostels, boarding houses, guest houses and serviced apartments are also taxed at 10%.

## 5. Stamp Duties on Property

All stamp duties are computed on the higher of the price or the market value, rounded down to the nearest dollar.

### 5.1 Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD), from 15 Feb 2023

| Portion of price or market value | Residential | Non-residential |
|---|---|---|
| First $180,000 | 1% | 1% |
| Next $180,000 | 2% | 2% |
| Next $640,000 | 3% | 3% |
| Next $500,000 | 4% | 4% |
| Next $1,500,000 | 5% | 5% |
| Remaining amount (above $3,000,000) | 6% | 5% |

### 5.2 Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD), residential, from 27 Apr 2023

| Buyer | 1st property | 2nd property | 3rd and subsequent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Singapore Citizen | 0% | 20% | 30% |
| Singapore PR | 5% | 30% | 35% |
| Foreigner | 60% | 60% | 60% |
| Entity / trustee | 65% | 65% | 65% |

ABSD is paid on top of BSD. Remissions and refunds exist, including:
- SC married couples buying a second property: ABSD refunded if they sell the first property within 6 months of the new property's TOP/CSC (or purchase date for a completed property).
- Single SC aged 55 and above buying a second residential property (purchases on or after 16 Feb 2024): refund available if conditions are met (details not researched).
- Nationals and PRs of certain Free Trade Agreement countries are treated like SCs. IRAS confirms this for US citizens. The full list (commonly cited as US, Switzerland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Iceland) is unverified in this research.

No change to ABSD rates after 27 Apr 2023 was found.

### 5.3 Seller's Stamp Duty (SSD), residential

Purchases on or after 4 Jul 2025 (holding period counted from the date the Option to Purchase is accepted):

| Sold within | SSD rate |
|---|---|
| 1 year | 16% |
| More than 1 and up to 2 years | 12% |
| More than 2 and up to 3 years | 8% |
| More than 3 and up to 4 years | 4% |
| More than 4 years | None |

Purchases from 11 Mar 2017 to 3 Jul 2025:

| Sold within | SSD rate |
|---|---|
| 1 year | 12% |
| More than 1 and up to 2 years | 8% |
| More than 2 and up to 3 years | 4% |
| More than 3 years | None |
